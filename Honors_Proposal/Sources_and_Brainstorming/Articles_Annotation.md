Daly, Matthew*. ColorCode: A Bayesian Approach to Augmentative and Alternative*

*Communication with Two Buttons.* 

	Throughout this research, Matthew Daly seeks to find a new AAC device for individuals facing severe mobility issues. This device will use the Bayesian formula to create an effective language tool, consisting of a red and blue button. These buttons sort and hypothesize the user's desired keyboard character, through a program named ColorCode.

	  
The main findings concluded that ColorCode has been tested to hypothesize a low rate of errors, and number of options(“clicks”) to generate the correct keyboard symbol, at a 2.07cpc(clicks per character). This device will infer symbols using Bayesian logic and prior inputs, as a way to quicken ColorCodes efficiency, and lower the effort needed by the user. These findings also compared and contrasted ColorCode to other popular AAC devices for individuals with limited mobility, such as Dasher, Shuffle Speller, and Nomon.

This study informs my project by showing how probability functions can be used throughout code and process creation. This study bridges together my knowledge of Logic and Algorithms and Statistics, with Accessibility and UX design. Having these specific functions added into your code not only eliminates much of the “guessing and checking” aspect, but ensures your code runs correctly throughout different calls and processes(a topic we’ve been discussing through pthreads in my ITSC 3146 class).

Briegal, Hunter. Pagal, Maya. Culpepper, J Shane. *IR for AAC Users: A Hyperdimensional Computing (Vector Symbolic Architectures) Approach.* ACM SIGIR 2025\. [https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3726302.3730273](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3726302.3730273) 

This research seeks to make more affordable and global AAC software/programs in efforts to spread autonomy and provide resources in lower income areas.	One of the challenges in cost efficient AAC devices is the high level of personalization for each user, making it impossible to deliver one generic AAC device to meet every user's wants and needs. The research suggests the use of Vector Symbolic Architectures to promote quality, affordable AAC devices.

The main findings consist of HDC being able to sort symbols and data for a user, in order to make them more accessible/easier to find. This is done using Hash-tables, bundling, binding and permutation to group vectors together. In this way, users have personalized groupings to coincide with their memory, emotions, connections, and feelings towards certain people, places, and objects.

This informs my project by once again, connecting information learned in my Logic and Algorithms class to real world coding examples. In addition, this information shows how A.I. and probabilistic models can be used to support independence and autonomy for individuals using AAC devices.

DISABILITY AND REHABILITATION: ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGY  
2018, VOL. 13, NO. 8, 731–739  
[https://doi.org/10.1080/17483107.2017.1369592](https://doi.org/10.1080/17483107.2017.1369592) 

The research question is how to create a mobile application for AAC users. Current mobile applications offer a more generic approach, while individualized speech would offer greater independence and communication for PwCD(People with Communication Disabilities). These devices and their use for PwCD cannot be thoroughly monitored with current insurance coverage, leading their beneficial aspects to be thoroughly time-constrained. “The extant AAC systems cannot fill this gap because clinician cannot monitor the PwCD’s perform-  
ance at home, which is related to the third limitation.” Lastly, these devices do not analysis and produce data on AAC usage in a reliable and fast manner. However, mHealth(mobile health) applications can mitigate or diminish these constraints.

The main findings of the mobile app EuTalk were overall positive, with training modules provided, and resources with a game-like design to promote activity in individuals. Data is collected and easily accessed through graphics and reports. The studies were conducted with 4 PwCD and 50 individuals without CD. The main critique was grammar and exhaustion from the study. The caveat provided states those with more experience with AAC had less critiques on grammar. This app provides options for custom buttons, promoting individuality in it’s design.

This study shows me the current gaps in AAC development(mainly affordability and individuality). With this information, I feel inclined to use my Front-End development skills to make personalized files that can be inputted into these programs, focusing on users' passions/interests. The game-like effect shows how AAC devices can provide a sort of incentive/excitement to users, while the user reports provide useful information for individuals, their caregivers, and doctors.
